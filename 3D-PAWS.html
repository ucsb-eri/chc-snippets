3D-Printed Automatic Weather Station

Through the Enhancing Meteorological Networks Partnership (EMNP), the UCSB Climate Hazards Center, in collaboration with the University Corporation for Atmospheric Research (UCAR) COMET's Innovative Capacity Development Program (ICDP) and the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET), is working with national meteorological agencies across Africa to support the deployment of 3D-Printed Automatic Weather Stations (3D-PAWS). These low-cost, locally manufactured stations provide real-time weather observations that strengthen drought and food security monitoring, weather forecasting, and early warning systems. The 3D-PAWS stations feature multiple temperature sensors, relative humidity sensor, wind speed and wind direction sensors, and two rain gauges. The dual rain gauge design allows for cross-checking of rainfall measurements and improved data reliability in operational networks. More information on the design and installation can be accessed here.

3D-PAWS Country Dashboards

The 3D-PAWS Dashboard provides interactive access to rainfall observations collected by this growing network of weather stations. Users can explore station locations, view rainfall measurements from the two on-site rain gauges, and compare these observations with satellite rainfall estimates.

  • 3D-PAWS Malawi
  • 3D-PAWS Zimbabwe

Using the Dashboard

Select a station on the map to view rainfall observations from that location. The accompanying graph displays rainfall recorded by the station's two rain gauges, rg1 and rg2, alongside CHIRP and NASA's IMERG satellite rainfall estimates and the CHPclim1 rainfall climatology. Comparing 3D-PAWS rain gauge data with satellite products helps users assess rainfall conditions and identify potential data quality issues.

Selecting a station automatically updates the rainfall comparison graph for that location. The dashboard URL also updates with the selected country and station, allowing users to bookmark or share a direct link to a specific station.

3D-PAWS Resources

The 3D-PAWS station network has expanded across Kenya, Ethiopia, Somalia, Malawi, Zimbabwe, and Madagascar, providing valuable real-time weather observations for climate monitoring and early warning applications. Rainfall observations from the Kenya 3D-PAWS network are now incorporated into the CHIRPS data stream, improving the density of available station observations used in rainfall monitoring.

These stations have also provided additional sources of corroboration. When each gauge records similar extremes, they provide added confidence that rainfall was truly exceptional as was in the case of very low rainfall values in eastern Kenya during the October to December 2025 short rain season.
